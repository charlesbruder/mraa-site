The 1973–74 Season: Big Eight Champions

An up-and-down fall, then a spring that brought the first Big Eight rugby championship home to Columbia.

The fall of 1973 was the club's first without coach Dr. Sterling Hayden, who retired after completing his doctorate, and it swung between brilliance and frustration on the way to a 5–5 record. The season opened with a 14–12 loss to the Kansas City Rugby Club decided by a disputed late penalty-kick call, followed by a 14–6 win over the St. Louis Ramblers. At the 32-team Heart of America Tournament in Kansas City's Swope Park — played before crowds of more than 2,000, with Scottish bagpipers on the sidelines — Missouri crushed Oklahoma State 23–0 before falling 3–0 to Minnesota on a late penalty kick. The high point came November 3, when the Tigers hammered arch-rival Rolla 30–4 at Reactor Field. Club president Rick Wetzel's verdict on the fall: “We just lacked consistency.”

The spring of 1974 was another story. After opening with a 24–0 shutout of Kansas City's River Quay club behind a John Loesing hat trick, Missouri hosted the Big Eight rugby tournament at Reactor Field on March 2–3. The Tigers blanked Kansas State 34–0, beat Nebraska 16–4, and then held off Iowa State 19–18 in a championship match played before a roaring home crowd — a final that featured Rick Wetzel landing a rarely-seen drop goal and Ed Sweeney scoring twice. Former coach Hayden, refereeing the tournament, called it the best rugby he had ever seen a Missouri side play.

The Tigers went on to take third at the 16-team Rolla invitational, win at Arkansas 16–4, and — after being controversially left out of the St. Louis Easter tournament bracket — beat two St. Louis clubs in exhibition play to prove the point. A 9–4 spring gave the club its most successful season yet, with the Big Eight trophy as its centerpiece.

Big Eight Champions

The men who brought the first Big Eight rugby title home to Columbia, March 1974.

Members of the championship squad, as listed in the Post-Dispatch: Mike Myrick, Rick Wetzel, John Loesing, Denny De Jong, Jon Brandt, Bill Winkler, John Dundore, Pat Costigan, Tom Siebolt, John Tarpoff, Ed Sweeney, Ben Calloway, Wally Fuetz, Scott Snyder, and Red Campbell. So many players turned out that Missouri entered two teams in its own tournament.

“The best organized tournament we've had the pleasure to participate in.”

Letter from the Kansas State Rugby Football Club to tournament organizer Rick Wetzel, March 5, 1974
